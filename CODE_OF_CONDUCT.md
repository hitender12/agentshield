# Code of Conduct

## Our pledge

We pledge to make participation in AgentShield a harassment-free
experience for everyone, regardless of age, body size, visible or
invisible disability, ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender identity
and expression, level of experience, education, socio-economic status,
nationality, personal appearance, race, caste, colour, religion, or
sexual identity and orientation.

We pledge to act and interact in ways that contribute to an open,
welcoming, diverse, inclusive, and healthy community — one where a
first-time contributor proposing a policy example is treated with the
same respect as a long-time maintainer reviewing the policy engine.

## Our standards

Examples of behaviour that contributes to a positive environment:

- Demonstrating empathy and kindness toward other people.
- Being respectful of differing opinions, viewpoints, and experience.
- Giving and gracefully accepting constructive feedback, especially on
  security-critical code, where hard questions are part of the work.
- Accepting responsibility and apologising to those affected by our
  mistakes, and learning from the experience.
- Focusing on what is best for the community and for the people who
  will rely on AgentShield's security properties — not on winning an
  argument.
- Backing claims with evidence: a test, a trace, an `explain` output,
  or a reference to the threat model, rather than an assertion.

Examples of unacceptable behaviour:

- The use of sexualised language or imagery, and sexual attention or
  advances of any kind.
- Trolling, insulting or derogatory comments, and personal or political
  attacks.
- Public or private harassment, including intimidation, stalking, or
  unwanted contact.
- Publishing others' private information, such as a physical or email
  address, without their explicit permission.
- Misrepresenting a contribution's security properties — for example,
  claiming a change makes AgentShield "production-secure" or protects
  against an attack the threat model says it does not.
- Knowingly submitting a policy, example, or test that contains a real
  credential, or asking someone else to share one.
- Other conduct which could reasonably be considered inappropriate in
  a professional setting.

## Security discussions

AgentShield is a security tool, and disagreement about its security
properties is expected and welcome — in the right place. Discuss
potential weaknesses in issues and pull requests, but **do not post
a live vulnerability publicly**. Report it privately following
[SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md) so it can be fixed before it is disclosed.
A reporter who follows that process will be thanked, not punished,
even if the report turns out to be a false alarm or a known limitation
already recorded in [THREAT_MODEL.md](THREAT_MODEL.md).

## Enforcement responsibilities

Community leaders and maintainers are responsible for clarifying and
enforcing the standards in this document, and for taking appropriate
and fair corrective action in response to any behaviour they deem
inappropriate, threatening, offensive, or harmful. They may remove,
edit, or reject comments, commits, code, wiki edits, issues, and other
contributions that are not aligned with this Code of Conduct, and may
communicate the reasons for moderation decisions when appropriate.

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies within all community spaces for
AgentShield — the repository, its issues, pull requests, and
discussions — and also applies when an individual is officially
representing the community in public spaces, for example when
presenting AgentShield at a conference or writing about it as a
maintainer or contributor.

## Reporting

If you experience or witness unacceptable behaviour, report it to the
project maintainers privately (open an issue only if the report
contains no sensitive information, and never include a vulnerability
in a conduct report — use the [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md) process for
that). All complaints will be reviewed and investigated promptly and
fairly, and the maintainers will respect the privacy and security of
the person who reported the incident.

## Enforcement guidelines

Community leaders will follow these guidelines when determining the
consequences for any action they deem in violation of this Code of
Conduct:

1. **Correction** — Use of inappropriate language or other behaviour
   deemed unprofessional or unwelcome. A private, written warning
   from community leaders, with clarity around the nature of the
   violation and an explanation of why the behaviour was
   inappropriate. A public apology may be requested.
2. **Warning** — A violation through a single incident or a series of
   actions. A warning with consequences for continued behaviour. No
   interaction with the people involved, including unscheduled
   interaction with those enforcing the Code of Conduct, for a
   specified period of time.
3. **Temporary ban** — A serious violation of community standards,
   including sustained inappropriate behaviour. A temporary ban from
   any sort of interaction or public communication with the community
   for a specified period of time.
4. **Permanent ban** — A pattern of violation of community standards,
   including sustained inappropriate behaviour, harassment of an
   individual, or aggression toward or disparagement of classes of
   individuals. A permanent ban from any public interaction within
   the community.
